How the matching engine works

Built for UK businesses: dates are read as DD/MM/YYYY and amounts are in pounds sterling (£).

  1. Reference match: same amount and a shared reference within 60 days. References come from any column tagged Reference (Invoice No., Cheque No., Doc No…) and from numbers in the description, so INV1043 on the bank matches Invoice No. 1043. This catches cheques that clear late.
  2. Amount and date: same amount within the date tolerance. Ties are broken by closeness of date and similarity of description.
  3. Same-customer/supplier batches: one bank payment that settles any number of cash book entries for the same company (e.g. a customer paying 8 invoices at once). It uses the Customer / Supplier column if there is one, otherwise the description, and finds the exact combination that adds up, from entries dated up to 45 days before the payment.
  4. Mixed batches: one bank line equal to the sum of 2–3 cash book lines from different payers within the tolerance (or the other way round), e.g. several cheques paid in on one deposit slip.
  5. Suggestions (never auto-applied): transposition errors (digits swapped, so the difference is divisible by 9), the same amount outside the date window, and similar descriptions with small amount differences.
  6. Statement: adjusted bank balance = statement balance + outstanding lodgements − unpresented payments. Adjusted cash book = cash book balance ± items only on the bank statement ± error corrections. The two should agree.
